The scene

Why this scene

Harry Potter is my all-time favorite movie, and this classroom scene is one of the funniest. A teacher walks the class through levitation: the wrist movement, the swish and flick, the careful way to say the spell. Then one student corrects another, gets challenged to do it better, and gets it right.

That sequence is mostly about how words are said, so it suits typography. The animation puts the emphasis, the mistake and the stubbornness on the screen as moving text.

Storyboard

Planning each beat

Before opening After Effects I planned one motion idea for every line of the scene. They run in the order they appear in the video.

  1. 1

    Opening

    A fade-in for a smooth start.

  2. 2

    Text from the wand

    The words slide out of the wand so they look like magic.

  3. 3

    Wrist movement

    A hand holding a wand moves together with the text, matching the line about wrist movement.

  4. 4

    The wand reveals

    The wand moves down and uncovers the text, guiding the audience to where they should look.

  5. 5

    Swish and flick

    A glowing effect, with magical particles fading in and out.

  6. 6

    "Besides, you're saying it wrong"

    The word "wrong" gets crossed out for a stronger visual effect.

  7. 7

    "It's Leviosa"

    A zoom-in between the letters stresses the pronunciation.

  8. 8

    The mispronunciation

    A slide-in translation, with the "SAR" part underlined to point out the mistake.

  9. 9

    "You do it then!"

    A background color change, plus pop-up and slide transitions so each word lands clearly.

  10. 10

    Stubbornness

    A pop-and-zoom on the text to show the stubborn attitude.

  11. 11

    The feather

    A split-slide layout, like reading a book, while the feather starts to fly.

  12. 12

    Close

    A fade-out to end the sequence smoothly.

Step by step

Building it in After Effects

The main techniques behind the motion, with the settings I used.

Setup

Sound, background and the first zoom

I attached the audio first, then added a rectangle shape as the background. After placing the video, a scale animation zooms out and transitions into a new background.

Title

Blur, flicker and evaporate

The title uses a Gaussian Blur so it fades away when it's no longer needed, with a flicker-in and an evaporate-out text animator. I adjusted scale and opacity to finish it.

Transitions

Circle wipes

To move between slides I used a circle shape that scales, so each scene zooms out into the next. It builds a little anticipation for what comes after.

Text animators

Range Selector in motion

Most lines slide in with a Range Selector. Start goes from 0% to 100%, and Offset goes from -100% to 100%. "Don't forget" uses an End keyframe from 0% to 87%.

Camera

Depth for "Swish and Flick"

I added a camera through a new solid layer and animated only its position, from 962, 515, -2610.67 to -962, 515, -2897.66. The "Swish and Flick" layer is copied and repeated.

Echo

Repeating the spell

"Wingardium Leviosa" repeats with scale and opacity keyframes to give an echo. I copied the layer and offset it in time, then added a slide, a Gaussian Blur of 81.4 and an opacity change.

Hand and wand

Rotation that feels natural

The hand and wand move up and down using position and rotation keyframes. Several keyframes at different angles make the motion feel less mechanical.

Emphasis

STOP, the eye and the crossed-out word

"STOP!" is a copied layer with a shape layer behind it for a different color, scaled for a zoom. "Someone's eye out" slides in with an eye element added on a position keyframe. "Wrong" gets a line shape drawn with Trim Paths, End from 0% to 100%.

Finish

Blur Fade In and the feather

"Leviosa" uses an Effects > Blur Fade In with a slider keyframe at 100, and the wrong version gets the same treatment. The feather follows a tutorial and is the last piece of the animation.

LeviosaR

The detail I like most

The distorted "R"

In the scene a student pronounces the spell wrong, so I gave the extra letter its own animation. It pops in much bigger than the text around it and wobbles to a stop.

Why it feels right

Animation principles

  • Slow in and slow out

    Several keyframes on the hand movement make it accelerate and settle naturally, so it looks smoother and more appealing.

  • Anticipation

    Zooming through a circle into each new background tells the viewer something is about to happen.

  • Timing and spacing

    Staggered keyframes control how fast or slow each line appears. The spacing gives a playful, magical rhythm that matches the scene.

  • Follow through

    The scale and rotation of the distorted "R" continue slightly past the main motion before settling.

  • Color

    Soft, dark tones in the backgrounds reduce distraction and keep the text easy to read.

  • Exaggeration

    Pushing the key moments makes the comedy stronger and helps the audience see the mistake and enjoy it.
